## Principle

The distributor is a replaceable pipe. BVS owns the artist relationship, Rights Passport, Studio, BeatStore, Radio, Zimbabwe guidance, and money visibility.

Core question: how does a musician operate in Zimbabwe — not how to become DistroKid.

## Why a Zimbabwean artist would pay BVS

Not because BVS is $1 cheaper than DistroKid. Because BVS is the operating system: Rights Passport, editorial radio, BeatStore, services, Paynow/EcoCash-shaped checkout, and a person who can explain the fragmented local economy. A $24/year self-serve distro does not do that.

Distribution royalty margin is **unlikely** to be the meaningful P&L line at current catalogue scale. More important: Premium, Instant/release fees, BeatStore, services, then radio/ads later.

## Stay vs migrate triggers

**Stay on an Amuse-class private/staff pipe** while:

- Paying Premium artists well under ~25–40
- Staff can still upload packs by hand
- Active catalogue << 2,000 releases
- No proven Zimbabwe payout on a white-label vendor
- Artists never see the pipe name

**Reassess API white-label (RFP + $1 ZW payout test, not a subscription)** when several of:

1. Premium cash covers ~$1k/yr infrastructure without a royalty tax
2. Studio → distribution jobs needs an API
3. BVS must appear as the label; pipe invisible
4. Amuse artist-cap or 25% lapse fee starts to bite
5. A real Zimbabwe payout actually lands
6. Written 0% (or known %) plus export/DDEX portability

## Recommendation

Keep current staff distribution pipe. Do not migrate. Reopen only when a trigger above is true.
